## Concept A: "Die Bohne rollt ins Wort"

**In one sentence:** a single coffee bean rolls in from the edge, stops, and turns into the letter "o" of the name, and the name locks up around it.

**First seconds (0–3 s), concretely**
- Picture: a flat warm paper ground; a single dark bean-shaped disc sits off-frame left.
- Text: none in the first second.
- Motion: the bean rolls right along the bottom third (physical roll, no slide), decelerates at center frame.
- Sound: deliberate silence, then one soft wooden tick on the stop (optional; sound-off safe without it).
- Hook mechanism: visual surprise (a shape that rolls like an object, not a logo fading in).

**The story** (4–5 beats, about 5 s)
1. 0.0–1.0 s: bean rolls in from the left (acts: rolls; reacts: nothing yet).
2. 1.0–2.0 s: bean stops at center and wobbles once (acts: stops; reacts: a letter-slot outline appears where it stops).
3. 2.0–3.2 s: bean becomes the "o" of BOHNENWERK; the other letters slide in from the sides in sequence, each pushed by the previous (cause and effect).
4. 3.2–4.5 s: lockup complete; one short settle with overshoot on the full word.
5. 4.5–5.5 s: ambient idle (1–2 % breathing scale) to the end frame, which holds the lockup.

**Open question that keeps people watching:** where the bean goes and what it becomes; answered at 2.0–3.2 s.

**Share trigger:** none strong. A logo sting is a recall piece; honest assessment: it is unlikely to be forwarded on its own. Possible: a café's own followers may repost it. No reach claim is made.
**Rewatch trigger:** the "o" in the lockup is the bean, visible only when the viewer knows the name; a second view shows the setup.

**Product / audience link:** the name only. No verified benefit. Audience: unverified (assumption: local café guests). Evidence: decisions.md.
**Recognition:** visual signature = flat paper ground, dark roast-tone disc, physical rolling motion. Recurring element = the bean (rolls in at the start, becomes a letter at the end). Brand elements used: none yet (provisional wordmark only; real logo file replaces it).

**Style:** Bauhaus (physical logic: a circle rolls, letters slide), adapted to the brand: 1 accent color; no gradients; no bounce on the bean (only the wordmark settles).
**Duration / format:** 5–6 s; 9:16 master, plus a square crop if the user later asks (not produced now).
**Effort:** S: GSAP timeline and SVG only.
**Asset needs:** exists: none. To create: provisional wordmark in a free-licensed open font (license to be confirmed before use), bean as an SVG path drawn in code. Paid: none.
**Main risks:** if the real logo has no bean-like element, the concept has to change; the "o" substitution must not distort the logo; roll physics must read at phone width.

## Concept B: "Die Anzeigetafel findet den Namen"

**In one sentence:** a split-flap departure board flips through a stream of random letters and locks on BOHNENWERK, the name arriving like a station announcement.

**First seconds (0–3 s), concretely**
- Picture: a dark board grid of about 12 cells in a single row, cells blank.
- Text: flaps flip through random letters; no readable word yet.
- Motion: cells flip cascading left to right with overshoot on each settle.
- Sound: mechanical flutter, one station chime at the lock (sound-off safe without it).
- Hook mechanism: curiosity gap (the flaps are running, the word is unknown).

**The story** (4–5 beats, about 6 s)
1. 0.0–1.0 s: cells flutter; letters cycle (acts: flip; reacts: each cell settles in order).
2. 1.0–3.5 s: the board cycles; cells lock one by one onto B-O-H-N-E-N-W-E-R-K, each lock triggering the next (cause and effect).
3. 3.5–4.5 s: the last cell locks; a single chime (the recurring sound motif).
4. 4.5–6.0 s: board holds with a short ambient idle; the brand wordmark (provisional) replaces the board, or the board stays as the logo.

**Open question that keeps people watching:** which word the board will land on; answered at 3.5 s.

**Share trigger:** none strong; the chime motif is recall, not a share trigger.
**Rewatch trigger:** the flutter ends in the exact name at a speed the first view cannot track; rewatch shows the cell order.

**Product / audience link:** the name only. Audience: unverified.
**Recognition:** visual signature = dark board with pale characters; recurring element = the station chime (opens and closes the piece).

**Style:** Split-Flap, adapted to the brand: dark plates, one pale color; no extra accent.
**Duration / format:** 6 s; 9:16 master.
**Effort:** M: per-cell CSS 3D flips with GSAP; the sound design needs free SFX.
**Asset needs:** exists: none. To create: per-cell flip module, a free or licensed mechanical flutter and chime (no paid generation; source to be checked). Paid: none.
**Main risks:** the flip cycle reads as a render glitch if too fast; the board is busy at phone width and may hide the real logo; sound-off viewers lose the chime, the board must still read.

## Concept C: "Der Kaffeering, der sich zum Namen rastert"

**In one sentence:** a coffee-ring stain spreads across a halftone dot field, and the dots darken into the name, as if the café's own mark were printed on the surface.

**First seconds (0–3 s), concretely**
- Picture: an off-white ground with a faint halftone dot screen in one brand ink.
- Text: none in the first second.
- Motion: a ring shape (a coffee-cup ring) appears at the bottom and expands once, fading to a light edge.
- Sound: none in the first second.
- Hook mechanism: everyday relevance (the ring is the most familiar café sign; the viewer recognizes the setting).

**The story** (4–5 beats, about 5 s)
1. 0.0–1.2 s: the ring appears and expands (acts: spreads).
2. 1.2–2.5 s: the dot screen under the ring grows darker (dot size animates; acts: develops).
3. 2.5–3.8 s: the dots resolve into the letters BOHNENWERK inside the ring (reacts: the ring is the frame).
4. 3.8–4.8 s: a fine ink-wipe finishes the lockup.
5. 4.8–5.5 s: ambient idle, the ring slowly dries (fades) into the end frame.

**Open question that keeps people watching:** what the ring will turn into; answered at 2.5 s.

**Share trigger:** none strong. A "that's so us" moment only applies if the café's own guests recognize the ring. Not claimed.
**Rewatch trigger:** the ring is the frame of the name; a second view shows that the ring's edge becomes the letter outline.

**Product / audience link:** the name only. Audience: unverified.
**Recognition:** visual signature = halftone dots in one ink; recurring element = the ring shape (appears at the start, frames the lockup at the end).

**Style:** Halftone, adapted to the brand: a two-ink palette where one ink is a placeholder brand color; no additional accent.
**Duration / format:** 5–6 s; 9:16 master.
**Effort:** S–M: SVG dot pattern plus GSAP; an effect on the image layer only if a raster is used.
**Asset needs:** exists: none. To create: a dot-pattern SVG and a coffee-ring SVG drawn in code; provisional wordmark. Paid: none.
**Main risks:** halftone dots can moiré at compressed mobile playback (use a coarser screen); the coffee-ring can read as a stain, not a brand element; the most subtle of the three, so weakest in a feed.

## C4. Recommendation and selection

Recommended: **Concept A.** It has the clearest sound-off build-up to a lockup, the lowest production effort (S), and
its recurring element (the bean) is a motif the name itself suggests. Trade-off: it depends on a bean motif
the café has not confirmed; if the real logo has no bean-like shape, A changes. B has the strongest sound
motif but needs audio sourcing and may hide the logo. C is calm but the least distinctive.
