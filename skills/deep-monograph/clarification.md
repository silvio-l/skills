# Clarification

This phase runs before any research or drafting. It has two parts: build the intake picture from what's already available, then close only the decisions that would genuinely change the topic, scope, subject, or usable outcome of the result. Nothing past this file's confirmation gate happens until the user has explicitly signed off.

## Intake schema

The following fields describe the assignment. The user does not have to fill this out themselves — it is the internal schema you resolve, from context first and questions second:

```
TOPIC:
[mandatory — either given directly or to be determined from the user's request]

CENTRAL QUESTION OR DESIRED READER OUTCOME:
[optional]

PARTICULAR EMPHASES:
[optional]

DELIBERATELY OUT OF SCOPE:
[optional]

REGIONAL, LEGAL, OR TECHNICAL CONTEXT:
[optional]

DEPTH LEVEL:
[automatic | thorough article | compact monograph | small book]

PRODUCTION MODE:
[automatic | single response | agentic workspace]

CURRENCY CUTOFF DATE:
[optional; default: the actual time research is performed]

SOURCES THAT MUST BE CONSIDERED:
[optional; URLs, videos, documents, files, studies, notes, or other material]

ROLE OF THE SUPPLIED SOURCES:
[set the topic | supply a starting thesis | to be critically checked | to be considered as supplementary]

DESIRED SOURCE TYPES:
[optional]

UNDESIRED OR EXCLUDED SOURCES:
[optional]

SPECIAL REQUIREMENTS:
[optional]
```

## Step 1 — self-extract before asking anything

Determine as much of the schema as possible, in this order, before formulating a single question:

1. the current task description,
2. the prior conversation context,
3. any supplied links, files, videos, or other material,
4. decisions already made earlier in the conversation,
5. only then, targeted questions.

Never re-ask for information that is already recoverable or was already answered earlier. Empty optional fields are not, by themselves, a reason to ask — fill them with a sensible default assumption as long as doing so doesn't materially change the outcome.

## Step 2 — ask only genuinely open, outcome-critical decisions

Run a structured interview only for decisions that would materially change the emphasis, scope, investigation target, or practical usefulness of the result. Ask **exactly one question at a time.**

Every decision question should contain:

1. the concrete decision to be made,
2. a short explanation of its consequences,
3. clearly distinguishable answer options,
4. your professionally reasoned recommendation,
5. an explicit invitation for a free-text answer instead of picking an option.

Avoid generic or unnecessary questions. Do not ask for anything that is already reliably derivable from the assignment, the context, or the supplied sources.

## Step 3 — classify every remaining gap into one of three types

**Uncritical gap.** Make a plausible assumption on your own and continue — no need to surface it.

**Several plausible readings with limited impact.** Pick the most probable and sensible reading. Note the assumption briefly and transparently later (e.g. in the methodology section), rather than interrupting for it now.

**Outcome-critical unclarity.** Ask exactly one targeted question, following the Step 2 shape, whenever different answers would fundamentally change the topic, the investigative direction, or the result.

## Step 4 — confirm-and-wait gate (hard block)

Once everything necessary is settled, summarize the shared understanding — compact but complete. The summary must cover at least:

- topic,
- central leading question,
- desired reader outcome,
- target audience,
- relevant emphases,
- deliberate exclusions,
- depth level,
- intended production mode,
- special sources and their role,
- currency requirements,
- planned output form.

Then explicitly ask for confirmation.

**Do not begin full research or drafting before this confirmation arrives.** After it does, proceed autonomously and only interrupt again for a newly discovered contradiction that is itself genuinely outcome-critical — not for routine judgment calls, which fall back to Step 3's "uncritical" or "limited impact" handling.
