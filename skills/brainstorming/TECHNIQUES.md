# Techniques — Fixed Catalogs for `brainstorming`

Disclosed reference for [`SKILL.md`](SKILL.md). These catalogs are applied in full, never as a pick-a-few menu — the completion criterion at each step is "every entry produced a concept," not "a sample."

## Inversions

Apply all ten to the framing question:

1. What is the worst possible solution — and what does that reveal about the true constraint?
2. What is the exact opposite of the usual solution?
3. What happens when the system's central goal is reversed?
4. What happens when its core function is removed entirely?
5. What happens when user and system trade roles?
6. What happens when errors become the desired outcome?
7. What happens when scarcity is replaced with abundance?
8. What happens when automation is replaced with deliberate friction?
9. What happens when the system provokes instead of responding?
10. What happens when the system's weakness becomes its main function?

## Contradictions

For each pair, name the concrete mechanism that lets a single concept satisfy both sides at once — not a compromise between them:

1. Highly personalized, and fully anonymous
2. Extremely capable, and minimalist
3. Automated, and human
4. Playful, and free of points, leaderboards, or rewards
5. Intelligent, and deliberately imperfect
6. Chaotic, and reliable
7. Local, and collectively learning
8. Instant, and unhurried
9. Expert-grade, and childlike
10. Permanent, and disposable

## Radicality Tiers

Generate at least one concept per tier per pass:

1. Unusual but plausible
2. Clearly unconventional
3. Radical, market-changing
4. Apparently absurd, with a recognizable functional core
5. Barely feasible today, conceivable long-term

## Mutation Operators

SCAMPER- and TRIZ-flavored. Apply each to every concept selected for mutation:

1. Exaggerate it by a factor of ten.
2. Reduce it to its smallest functional core.
3. Remove its single most load-bearing assumption.
4. Transplant it into a completely unrelated context.
5. Cross it with another selected concept.
6. Redesign it for a user group it was never meant for.
7. Spin one of its features into a standalone system.
8. Turn its biggest flaw into its defining feature.

## Unstuck Card

When a step lands short of its completion criterion, or the same concept keeps returning in different clothes, draw one real card instead of forcing another synthetic variation:

```
~/.claude/skills/brainstorming/scripts/draw --category provocations --count 1
```

Apply the drawn instruction to the current concept literally, even where the literal reading feels wrong — the friction is the point.

## Surprise Test

A concept passes when all four hold. Discard it, with a one-line reason, the moment one fails:

- It changes an actual process, role, assumption, or business model — not a coat of paint on an existing one.
- It contains at least one moment a domain expert would first want to argue with.
- It reaches its outcome through a structurally different mechanism than the ten obvious answers gathered before drawing began.
- It carries a full descent chain (which lenses, collision cards, inversions, contradictions, or operators produced it) — a concept that can't name its lineage is indistinguishable from a generic suggestion and fails on that alone.
