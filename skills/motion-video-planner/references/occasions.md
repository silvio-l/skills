# Occasion playbook

Load this in phase C, and read only the section for the occasion at hand. Each section gives
what works, defaults, and traps. All of it sits on top of `creative-principles.md`.

## Explainer

- **Shape**: mystery or everyday problem → mechanism, shown as cause and effect → "aha" visual →
  takeaway. One idea per scene.
- **Defaults**: 45–90 s; voiceover with captions; the diagram *acts* (parts push, block, flow)
  instead of being labelled.
- **Traps**: definitions before relevance; text walls; abstract icons with nothing happening.

## Product video

- **Shape**: the problem is *experienced* (relatable situation, escalation) → the product enters
  as the surprising helper → the simplest real interaction → visible change → brand close.
- **Defaults**: 20–45 s; real UI captured or faithfully rebuilt from the repo (only shipped
  features); one hero feature, not a feature list.
- **Traps**: logo first; feature tours; fake UI or roadmap features; claims without evidence.

## Promotion (campaign, offer, event)

- **Shape**: hook tied to the offer's real benefit → reason to care now → offer details (exact,
  verified: price, date, conditions) → CTA.
- **Defaults**: 10–20 s, 9:16 or 1:1; the CTA is allowed to be explicit here.
- **Traps**: fake urgency (invented scarcity, countdowns to nothing); fine print missing or
  unreadable.

## Launch

- **Shape**: the world before (recognizable pain) → tease → reveal (product, version, feature) →
  one proof moment → where to get it.
- **Defaults**: 20–40 s plus optional 6–10 s cutdowns; the reveal carries the recurring element.
- **Traps**: a teaser with no reveal; "coming soon" for things that are not scheduled; launch
  dates not confirmed by the user.

## Logo animation / sting

- **Shape**: one idea that expresses the brand (a material, a motion, a sound) → build-up →
  lockup → short ambient hold.
- **Defaults**: 3–6 s; `/motion-graphics`; real logo file (SVG); exact brand colors; a 2–4-note
  sound motif if audio is wanted; variants for light and dark backgrounds, or transparent overlay
  (MOV with alpha) if it is meant to sit over footage.
- **Traps**: redrawing the logo; distorting proportions; generic particle storms with no relation
  to the brand; transparent output for a full-frame design.
- **No logo file**: if the logo *is* the subject and none exists, it is a blocker for the final
  render, not for planning. Plan with the wordmark set from a named font, mark it `user` (the
  real file) or as a `PROVISIONAL` wordmark the user must approve. Never redraw or invent a mark
  and present it as the brand's.
- **Share trigger**: a pure sting rarely has one. Say so honestly ("none strong; the goal is
  recall") instead of inventing one.

## Social clip

- **Shape**: a hook in frame one → a loopable or punchline-driven core → the end flows back into
  the start (a loop) or ends on the punchline.
- **Defaults**: 7–30 s; 9:16; sound-off safe (on-screen text); platform safe areas; fast pacing
  (about 1.5–3 s per idea).
- **Traps**: slow intros; text in the UI overlay zones; a CTA that interrupts the loop.

## Interactive formats (quiz, prediction, comparison, dilemma)

**A rendered video cannot receive viewer input.** Never fake interactivity: no "results" from
polls that did not happen, no "most people chose B". The video can invite a mental answer or a
comment, then reveal. Distinguish the four formats; they need different fairness rules.

### Knowledge quiz

- A question with **one verifiably correct answer** (source cited in the prompt) and **plausible
  distractors** (wrong for a checkable reason, not absurd).
- **Thinking time** fits the difficulty: about 3–6 s with a visible but non-gimmicky tension
  device (the options *react*: pulse, lean, wobble). Use a countdown only if it is the clearest
  way to signal the time window.
- **Resolution**: show *why* the answer is right (a mini-explanation or visual proof); the "aha"
  is the payoff. Optionally include a bonus fact.
- **Sound-off**: the question and every option are on screen as text for the whole thinking
  time and stay until the reveal has landed (they may shrink or move aside, not vanish); a voiceover may read them, but it must not be the only carrier.
- **Answer position**: do not always put the correct option first or last. Choose its position
  deliberately, and say in the prompt that it was chosen. The reveal must be readable without
  colour alone: add a check mark, strike-through, or movement.
- Series format: consistent layout, a recurring host element, a score that does not depend on
  viewers.

### Prediction ("What happens next?")

- Shows a setup, asks for the outcome, then reveals the **real, recorded or physically correct
  outcome**. Never stage a fake result.
- The outcome must be genuinely non-obvious but fair: all information needed is visible before
  the question.
- **Thinking time**: about 2–4 s, freeze or slow motion at the decisive moment.

### Comparison (A vs. B)

- **Equal footing**: same metric, same conditions, same scale, sources for every number.
- The reveal is the measured difference, visualized honestly (no truncated axes that exaggerate).
- If one side is the user's product, say so; avoid strawman competitors and never name a
  competitor without verified claims.

### Dilemma ("Would you rather / What would you do?")

- **No right answer.** Both options are fair, attractive, and costly in a way the audience feels.
- **Thinking time**: about 3–5 s; the options can "argue" visually.
- **Resolution**: show the consequences of each path, or hand the decision to the audience
  ("comment A or B"). No fake majority, no moralizing verdict unless the brand's message is
  exactly that and it is stated honestly as an opinion.

## Other occasions (recruiting, event recap, tutorial, internal update …)

Use the closest shape above, state why, and adapt the defaults. A tutorial is an explainer with
real UI; a recap is a montage that needs footage (an asset question); recruiting is a "that's so
us" piece about the team's real culture, with no invented employee quotes.
